Money rules worth following

Simple thumb rules, not laws of physics — but they hold up well over time.

50 / 30 / 20 rule

50% of income to needs, 30% to wants, 20% to savings/investing. Adjust upward on savings as income grows — the goal is a rising savings rate, not a fixed one.

Rule of 72

Divide 72 by your expected annual return to find years to double your money. At 12% return, money doubles roughly every 6 years.

Emergency fund first

Keep 6 months of expenses in a liquid, safe instrument before investing aggressively. This is what lets you stay invested when markets fall, instead of forced selling.

100 − age rule (equity allocation)

Rough guide: equity % of portfolio ≈ 100 − your age. At 30, ~70% equity is reasonable; the rest in debt/gold as ballast. Adjust for personal risk appetite.

Pay yourself first

Move savings/investment amount out on income day, before spending. Whatever's left is what you spend — not the other way around.

Avoid lifestyle inflation

When income rises, let savings rate rise with it — don't let expenses absorb the entire raise. This single habit is what separates compounding wealth from a rising-income treadmill.

Debt that builds vs. debt that drains

Debt for appreciating assets or income-generating skills (education, business) can be worth it. Debt for depreciating consumption (gadgets, lifestyle) rarely is.

Time in the market > timing the market

Staying invested through cycles has historically outperformed trying to predict tops and bottoms, especially for long-horizon goals like retirement.
